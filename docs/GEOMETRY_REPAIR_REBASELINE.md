# Geometry repair rebaseline — incremental, inspectable surface sculpting (2026-10-21)

## Product contract (normative for new repair work)

Mesher is a continuously evolving surface sculptor, **not** a source-certified exact Boolean tool. Keep V1–V12 immutable COW partitions, stable IDs, async 4D strokes, renderer, undo/redo and checkpoints; reuse V13–V16 triangle collision, winding, contour planning, cap/zipper primitives and quality tests. V17 exact-source Boolean remains available as a reference/legacy opt-in, but does **not** determine acceptance of ordinary approximate Merge repairs. V18 M07 GPU acceptance remains unaccepted; its global normal-only backtracking is **not** the intended general-purpose Merge-OFF contact-constrained deformation.

- **Topological health:** an ordinary accepted edited mesh must retain valid manifold connectivity (separately closed, orientable manifold components are allowed inside one root). Geometric self-intersections, particularly minor local residuals, do not inherently make this topology invalid. Extreme/ambiguous geometry need not be repaired automatically. Hard limits protect valid identities, finite coordinates and no partial publication.
- **No intentionally enclosed interior voids (global material model):** Mesher supports exposed recesses, through-holes and passages connected to the exterior, but never deliberately preserves sealed internal cavities. When sculpting severs the last passage to an inward-facing shell, a wholly enclosed disconnected inner boundary is redundant and must be retired as an entire component, with no extra cap or zipper for the component itself. This is NOT just a hollow-sphere-fixture exception, and is not conditional on that cavity being newly formed: already-sealed cavities are not a supported intentional feature. Separate outward-facing exterior solids are still allowed. Proof of safe deletion requires actual stable-ID isolation, sound closed component orientation, and reliable spatial containment/material-side evidence; orientation or bounding-box nesting alone is insufficient. Malformed or ambiguous unrelated imported geometry is not silently cleaned up. The prior V13–V19 historical non-goal about indiscriminate cavity removal remains an older, bounded safety statement, not authorization to preserve intentional sealed voids in this rebaselined product.
- **Merge ON:** apply the brush's proposed deformation, classify newly affected material boundary and remove buried geometry, then create approximate, density-aware caps or zipper connections based on existing edge loops. Aim for healthy edge distribution, local fidelity and structural manifoldness, not mathematically perfect sections. Minor *newly created* secondary crossings in an otherwise valid repair may persist and be managed by later stamps; report/track them rather than globally vetoing every edit. The original V15/V16 hard zero-residual-crossing admission must be reconsidered, not simply bypassed without an explicit structural check.
- **Merge OFF:** any current/future brush proposes an arbitrary 3D displacement field; a localized nonpenetration/clearance resolver clips only motion that would worsen or introduce penetration, smoothly conforming to obstacles with a locally scaled small gap. A global scalar reducing Inflate normals is inadequate. The unmodified existing geometry can already contain intersections and unrelated bad geometry should not veto edits.
- **Independent brushes:** Inflate is only the first producer. Geometry collision response and remeshing belong to the shared tail pipeline, not individual brush code. Smooth (curvature/neighborhood regularization, potential local self-intersection mitigation) is a *future feature*, not a prerequisite or immediate milestone.
- **Symmetry:** exact topological reflection is retained only while every accepted change continues it. The first accepted asymmetric operation transparently relinquishes that invariant; geometry remains in place and undo restores historical symmetry state. Re-enabling symmetric brush input afterward mirrors the *operation*, not vertex IDs: left/right geometry evolve independently. Do not infer topology correspondence from coordinate coincidence.
- **Multiple local repairs:** a stamp may create many intersection islands. Detect intersecting triangle pairs (an intersection need not pass through any original vertex), classify candidate removal faces, then derive actual directed excision boundaries. Intersection islands, connected excision patches and oriented boundary loops are distinct entities with many-to-many relationships. Never blindly pair nearest loops or assume one pair per stamp. One closed eligible loop may be capped; two compatible loops may be zippered or capped separately; closed redundant components may disappear with no loops. Several independent simple repairs can coexist in one transaction. Complex interconnected many-to-many (e.g. 3-vs-5), ambiguous pairing, branching/open contours or inadmissible repairs may refuse a stamp with a diagnostic. Equality of loop counts alone is never a sufficient pairing proof.
- **Staging:** temporary open-boundary intermediate states are inspection-only snapshots, not published normal closed mesh roots. The frozen debug pipeline never changes normal accepted mesh history or checkpoint. In production each accepted edit publishes at most one valid complete immutable root/history transition; a refused step leaves it intact. Finite budgets and honest diagnostics throughout.
- **Acceptance:** deliberate CPU positive/negative geometry tests and *physical* Linux/Windows Vulkan inspection. Include zero-crossings, interior/interior triangle intersections with no crossed vertices, multiple independent islands, one-sided excision, mixed cap/zipper, wrong-side removal, unrelated pre-existing intersections, both moving surfaces, ambiguous many-to-many, variable triangle densities, repeated evolving strokes and controlled residual crossings. Tests requiring a refusal do not prove positive reconstruction.

## Flat independently testable milestones (R01–R08)

These are chronological peer deliverables, not nested TODOs. Preserve historical milestone evidence and implement in separate reviewed commits/PRs. R01–R06 are deliberately **not yet useful for normal editing**: freeze on the first qualifying post-stamp event so a human can orbit/inspect a single deterministic candidate. Exit/relaunch to reset.

- **R01 — Post-stamp intersection freeze and visible markers.** Separate explicit developer CLI mode, with the regular production path unchanged. After complete Inflate/Refine/Collapse but before publishing, run a finite revision-local changed-neighborhood triangle-triangle BVH query. On newly detected intersecting/ambiguous pairs, after bounded comparison with the previous immutable root, freeze subsequent brush modification, retain the *uncommitted candidate* for a read-only viewport, and show red markers at every incident vertex and darker red attached edge geometry. Explain that these are incident elements, **not necessarily actual intersection points**. Include recorded FaceId pairs, query work, independent intersections and clean/stale cases; no deletion or vertex projection. Camera navigation and interior inspection remain available. The stored root/history/checkpoint remain the last accepted mesh; a candidate is never silently durable. Explicit finite query exhaustion freezes with a distinct error rather than falsely claiming a clean candidate. Acceptance includes physical Vulkan observation; do not mark complete from CPU alone.
- **R02 — Frozen vertex-star excision preview.** Take endpoints of each R01 detected contact triangle as conservative candidate vertices, include their complete incident-face fans, then add any extra retained pinch vertices needed for a **non-branching** directed cut boundary under explicit finite budgets. Include orphan vertices created by component-wide face retirement. Highlight the resulting hypothetical deleted faces, source vertices and oriented boundary loops *without* editing geometry. V14 buried/visible winding classification is **informational**, not a threshold for removing only isolated triangles; physical torus/hollow previews showed that such isolated per-face voting produced invalid point-branching cuts. Preserve separate independent regions and explain any required conservative halo expansion. A valid R02 cut is not a guarantee that a later cap/zipper is possible.
- **R03 — Frozen excision-only inspection.** Delete classified faces and orphaned vertices *privately*; intentionally open holes highlighted red. Confirm preview matches actual directed loops and unaffected IDs/geometry survive. These are not ordinary publishable closed mesh revisions; do not offer save/undo/stamp from this snapshot.
- **R04 — Forced zipper inspection.** `--repair zipper` on eligible paired loops only; new seam faces blue. Test multiple independent pairings, mismatched edge counts, correct winding and quality; explicitly refuse one-loop or many-to-many unsupported repair without silently capping.
- **R05 — Forced cap inspection.** `--repair cap` on eligible individual loops only; new cap faces green. Test single-hole, multiple independent caps, cavity/topology changes, orientations and nonplanar refusals.
- **R06 — Per-region automatic closure.** `--repair auto`: apply V15.1 fitness and evidence-based matching to choose caps, zippers, or no closure for truly redundant closed components, independently per admissible region. Identify completely enclosed redundant closed components using stable-ID isolation, winding/orientation and bounded containment evidence; intentionally preserving an enclosed air pocket is NOT a product mode. Mixed caps/zippers in one stamp. Ambiguous pairings (including 3-vs-5) refuse as a complete transaction; mere equal counts don't authorize sewing.
- **R07 — Continuous live repair.** Remove freeze; allow successive stamps while tracking changed/new/residual crossings, preserving structural manifoldness and minimizing quality degradation. Include bounded end-of-stroke stabilization, local re-repair and evidence that unrelated old intersections do not veto strokes. Revisit exact zero-residual gate and failure handling deliberately, not by deletion.
- **R08 — Production integration / physical acceptance.** Retire diagnostic-only CLI policies after GPU acceptance, enable auto approximate repair in normal Merge, integrate arbitrary brush proposals (separately planned Merge-OFF spatial contact resolver), exact-topology-to-independent brush symmetry handoff, persistent root/history/checkpoint/dirty renderer guarantees and Linux/Windows testing.

## Topology-changing closure acceptance fixtures (deferred positive tests)

Two independently observable **topological changes**, beyond ordinary two-protrusion contact, should be covered by R02–R07 (they do NOT expand R01 acceptance or introduce new prerequisite milestones).

1. **Torus central hole pinched shut.** Begin with a valid connected closed orientable torus (genus 1). Brush geometry toward itself around its central aperture until the material boundary should become a valid connected genus-0 closed surface. R01 detects newly affected independent triangle-pair contact; R02 shows which interior-facing portions would be retired and what boundary contours arise; R03 verifies the proposed excision yields only valid oriented boundary loops; R04/R05 verify their respective closure strategies on supported cases; R06 must choose the appropriate local closure without leaving a non-manifold singular pinch or a spurious surviving tunnel. Verify Euler characteristic changes from 0 to 2 *when the expected result is indeed a single sphere-like surface*, along with component count, orientation, and face quality. Unsupported ambiguous intermediate loop structures produce explicit refusal, not invented repair.
2. **Hollow shell mouth pinched shut; interior becomes redundant.** Start with a connected, closed genus-zero, finite-thickness shell: outward-wound outer sphere, inward-wound inner sphere and one connecting tube at the mouth. The inner shell is initially exposed to the outside *through that tube*. After the brush seals the opening, the enclosed inner shell becomes entirely redundant material-boundary geometry and must be **deleted as a complete component, without a cap or zipper**. The surviving exterior is one closed, outward-oriented genus-zero component, with no intentional internal void. Expected final component count is one and Euler characteristic is 2, not two components/4. Before closure, the mouth and inner shell must remain visible, topologically connected, and editable.

Neither fixture can be accepted by mere successful contact detection, equal contour counts, nor a final picture alone. Assess before/after occupancy, oriented boundaries, connected components and vertex-link manifoldness. Both might require unsupported many-to-many repair in some brush configurations; choose a deliberately supported fixture for early positive tests, then add an explicit ambiguous/refusal case. For the hollow shell, the key distinct positive case is **zero-loop closed-component deletion** after isolation, rather than a new surface closure operation. At the R07 stage repeat during continuous strokes and check that local residual crossings do not destroy these topological results.

**Detection-vs-excision distinction:** R01's red vertex/edge overlay is conservative: it includes all endpoints of contacting faces, even vertices that have not geometrically crossed the opposing sheet. As chosen for R02 after physical testing, those red endpoints are **the default deletion seeds**, with every incident face selected for removal. This intentionally removes a larger-than-minimal halo and can merge initially separate nearby cuts. R02 therefore also inspects the hypothetical cut graph, absorbs additional boundary-pinch vertices under finite limits, tracks orphans and refuses cases that cannot form simple oriented contours. V14 winding samples remain useful for later material reasoning but do not veto a geometrically valid incident-vertex proposal. R03 must prove the open-boundary geometry actually corresponds to this plan, with no unwanted disconnected remnants.

## R01 implementation boundary

R01 is **diagnostic only**, not the automatic repair engine. Intersections are triangle-pair relationships, not vertex tests. Visual vertex/edge markings identify potentially affected topology; R02 owns deciding which geometry to cut. The frozen candidate is a preview of the attempted post-remesh Step, not a newly committed revision. Preserve old-root integrity even if input commands were queued before freeze. Keep the existing V17/V18 paths and their tests intact; do not claim R01 hardware acceptance without actual GPU evidence.

## Developer starter geometry fixtures (independent of R01 and repair)

- `--torus`: exactly `--vertices N` randomized surface samples on the torus about Y-axis with major radius **0.55** and minor radius **0.45** (minimum radial hole **0.10**), stratified to match uniform *surface area*, relaxed tangentially, and triangulated periodically as a connected genus-one closed manifold. Torus topology must not be mistaken for a convex sphere or depend on a convex hull. The per-fixture pseudorandom seed and relaxation controls should be reproducible.
- `--hollow-sphere`: start with N/2 outer radius **1.0** and N/2 inner radius **0.95** sampled spheres (inward-facing inner normals). Delete vertices in the negative-Y side of the axial cylinder of radius **0.1**, giving a mouth in each. Add two tube rings at **Y = -0.96 (outer)** and **Y = -0.93 (inner)**, radius **0.095**, with a half-increment azimuth phase between rings. Zip outer mouth→outer tube ring, tube ring→tube ring, inner tube ring→inner mouth. The *final* vertex count exceeds N by the two connector-ring populations minus removed cap vertices. Validate orientation, a single manifold component, zero boundary edges, Euler characteristic 2, and a clear passage into the initial interior. These are *construction fixtures*, not promises of working automated repair.
- Both fixtures are inherently asymmetric when randomized. They start in independent-topology mode; they should not claim exact reciprocal mirror mappings merely because the default sphere configuration uses symmetry. The long-term user-facing brush-symmetry transition remains independently planned.
